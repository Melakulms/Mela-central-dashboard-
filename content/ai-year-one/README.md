# MELA AI Education — Year One

DRAFT FOR HUMAN TEACHER REVIEW — 11 October 2026

52 weekly units · 4 guided hours per week · 208 hours total. Each week contains an explanation, worked example, practical task, two checkpoint questions and four session activities. Weeks 12, 28, 36, 48 and 52 are major checkpoints. This is a common foundation with 53 catalogue-linked project briefs, not 53 independently completed year-long qualifications. Existing courses have not been overwritten or marked published.

Designed for secondary-age learners, TVET, university and adult beginners. Teachers must adapt language, prerequisites, difficulty and safeguarding. Younger learners need separate age-appropriate design. No paid AI account is required: teachers may supply fictional outputs and use paper tracing. Programming can be traced on paper when a computer is unavailable. The pace is a suggested teaching plan, not a claim that reading alone takes 208 hours.

All examples and tasks are original drafts using fictional data. No lesson or question has been approved by a human teacher. All reviewer fields remain empty. English is the draft language; the five platform-language editions require translation and native-speaker review.

Each week: 60 minutes guided explanation; 60 minutes practical work; 60 minutes peer verification and revision; 60 minutes teacher assessment and correction. Carry incomplete work forward rather than recording completion automatically.

## Assessment and publication gate

Teacher scores each artifact out of 20: conceptual correctness 0–4; practical execution 0–4; evidence and verification 0–4; privacy/safety/rights 0–4; independent explanation 0–4. Proposed progression threshold: 14/20 with no unresolved critical safety or factual error. Teachers must calibrate this proposal locally before adopting it. A critical safety failure blocks publication regardless of total. Retest after correction and retain both attempts.

Before publication, a verified subject teacher independently solves both checkpoint questions and completes the practical exercise. Check key correctness, acceptable variants, grade fit, source rights, cultural/language fairness and accessibility. Record reviewer identity, date, content version/hash, evidence, decision and corrections. A second reviewer checks disputed or high-stakes content. Automated checking cannot supply teacher sign-off.
